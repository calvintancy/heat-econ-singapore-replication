Crisis and recovery-window exclusions

These comparisons remove documented disruption periods and a separately labelled recovery buffer. They retain the full-history comparison and never drop a month because its observed growth or regression p-value is large. The estimand becomes the weather association in the retained months; the exclusions do not identify a causal effect or certify those months as free of shocks.

For growth, the entire interval from base month through observation month must avoid the excluded windows. Under the buffered rule the first retained MoM months are February 2011 and February 2024; first retained YoY months are January 2012 and January 2025. Levels only require the observation month to avoid the windows. Growth is computed before filtering, so no change is calculated across a deleted gap.

The source begins in 2008. The Asian financial crisis and SARS therefore lie outside this panel. The GFC exclusion cannot affect the full-control models, whose dates begin in 2017; identical COVID-only and GFC+COVID columns in that window are expected. A 2023 recovery buffer does not imply that 2024 was unaffected.

With the wider crisis/recovery exclusion, the long aggregate MoM model retains 139 months and estimates −0.078 growth log points per +0.1°C (nominal p=0.519). The short full-control model remains negative (−0.483, p=0.033; 55 months). Its pre-COVID sensitivity is near zero (−0.055, p=0.734), with only 32 months and 13 residual time degrees of freedom. The negative association is still sensitive to the window.

Why these calendar windows?

Singapore's recession began in 2008Q3, as described by PMO. The whole 2008–2009 window is a conservative calendar choice. MTI's historical tourism table records the large 2010 arrivals rebound. STB's 2023 review describes a strong recovery, with arrivals at about 71% of 2019. These motivate the separately labelled recovery years; they are not official exact crisis boundaries.
