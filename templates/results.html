The first specification

What the regression measures.

An equal-weight relationship between national weather and sector growth, using within-year temperature variation.

Quarterly sector panelgit = β Tt + γ1 Raint/100 + γ2 RHt + αi,season(t) + λyear(t) + εit
Outcome · real value-added growth
100 × [ln(VA at t) − ln(VA at t−1)], using seasonally adjusted SingStat levels in chained 2015 Singapore dollars. Quarterly growth is not annualised. Sector weights are equal.
Exposure · quarterly mean air temperature
Actual published monthly means at Changi Climate Station, averaged using the number of days in each month. Rainfall totals are summed; mean humidity is day-weighted. All three months must be available.
Fixed effects · sector × season and year
Each sector has its own quarter-of-year pattern. Year effects remove common annual conditions. Full year-quarter effects would absorb national temperature completely, making its common coefficient unidentified.
Inference · shared weather and serial dependence

A first association, with clear limits.

Quarterly macro demand and sector-specific shocks remain potential confounders. Year effects also remove annual temperature shifts, so this model does not identify permanent climate damage. A common Changi exposure cannot measure heat at individual workplaces. The equal-sector result is not an aggregate GDP effect. Unit-root evidence is mixed for some growth series; levels and untestable short/gapped windows are exploratory. Significance is nominal and does not establish stationarity. Full stationarity audit →

Sample construction and data vintages

The broad panel uses 11 non-overlapping industries. The finer splits begin in 2005Q1, so the 19-sector panel starts with quarterly growth in 2005Q2. Parent industries and their children are never included together. Ownership of dwellings, taxes and aggregate rows are excluded. Chain-linked sector values need not add to aggregate real GVA, so the sector levels are not summed into an aggregate.

Source data are credited to SingStat and NEA under the terms on the linked dataset pages. Download the full source manifest.

Connection to the earlier electricity-demand study

The earlier EMA study used half-hourly system demand, temperature bins, rainfall, wind, humidity and PM2.5, with calendar fixed effects and date-clustered standard errors. This VA project retains the weather-control and calendar-effect approach while adapting it to quarterly sector growth.

Quarterly temperature enters linearly. A long, consistent Changi series replaces the earlier average across active stations. Wind and PM2.5 are omitted from the long baseline because the earlier complete control sample covers only 2017–2025. The new model has an actual sector dimension.

Reference project: ema_empirical_note_aea_2026-03-08. Exact source paths and adaptation notes are saved in notes/prior_electricity_reference.md.

Independent verification and reproduction

Independent quarterly-mean OLS reproduces the panel temperature coefficient. An independent QR fit with explicit within-sector score sums verifies CR1 standard errors and t(S−1) intervals, using S=11 sectors. A direct Bartlett score-covariance calculation separately reproduces the calendar-HAC benchmarks, including the 12-quarter gap in the COVID exclusion. Sector CR1 uses S/(S−1) × (N−1)/(N−K); the calendar-HAC benchmark instead uses T/(T−1) × (N−1)/(N−K) and t(T−1), where T counts observed quarters and K includes all fixed-effect coefficients. Few-sector and short-time inference remain approximate.

In the project folder, run uv sync --locked, then uv run python scripts/run_all.py. Cached sources are reused. Add --refresh only to replace the source vintages and regenerate the analysis. Download verification details.
