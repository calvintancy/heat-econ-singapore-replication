Growth, weather histories and nonlinear heat effects

The published methods inform the timing and functional form of our regressions. This report identifies the exact paper/version, the implemented robustness and the limits of the Singapore adaptation. All coefficients use native PyFixest. Existing contemporaneous, growth and level results remain in the main report.

This is a post-exploration specification extension. A large grid and numerical agreement do not establish causality. The main scientific limits are common national exposure, omitted macro shocks, limited time replication, specification sensitivity and multiple testing.

Joint restrictions
Multi-restriction Wald inference is not calibrated for headline claims. Representative fixed-design Gaussian-noise checks, with true zero temperature effects, reject far more often than the nominal 5% rate. The unchanged VA12 long-history NSA-YoY distributed-lag H1 design (174 quarters) rejects 2,000 of 2,000 draws for its 60-coefficient joint null. This is a selected-design diagnostic, not a rejection-rate estimate for every table. Full covariance rank establishes computability only; it does not validate the reference F distribution. Treat the displayed multi-restriction Wald p-values as asymptotic diagnostics, and do not use them as headline evidence of heterogeneity or a mechanism. Inspect the design, seed, replay and adjudication.
These restrictions generally address different coefficient patterns, but some are identical in the restricted models. In the two-channel model, cumulative-zero and static-shape restrictions both test the growth-sum coefficient equal to zero; in annual DJO1 the lag-sum and static-level restrictions coincide. In a one-temperature benchmark or DJO0, the current coefficient and its sum coincide. Repeated aliases are not independent confirmation. The p-values use the full covariance matrix and are nominal conditional tests; failing to reject does not establish a mechanism.


YoY output growth does not dictate one temperature transformation
Quarterly YoY log growth is 100 × [ln Y(t) − ln Y(t−4)]. It is also the sum of four consecutive QoQ log changes. Monthly YoY uses twelve months. NSA output is our primary YoY definition, with SA retained as a comparison. A temperature level, a temperature difference and accumulated exposure encode different economic timing assumptions.

Five-temperature quarterly restrictions without lagged Y
YoY growth(t) = other terms + a0 T(t) + a1 T(t−1)
             + a2 T(t−2) + a3 T(t−3) + a4 T(t−4)

Contemporaneous static-level channel:
  a1 = a2 = a3 = 0 AND a0 + a4 = 0   [4 joint restrictions]

Equal contemporaneous quarterly-growth channel:
  a0 = a1 = a2 = a3 AND a4 = 0        [4 joint restrictions]

Both parsimonious channels:
  b × [T(t) − T(t−4)]
  + c × [T(t) + T(t−1) + T(t−2) + T(t−3)]

Unrestricted direct lag sum = a0 + a1 + a2 + a3 + a4.
A zero sum alone is weaker than the exact static-level restriction.
With lagged Y, this sum is not the unconditional long-run multiplier.
When lagged growth enters, cancelling endpoints and zero intermediate direct-weather coefficients describe a conditional coefficient shape only. They are not an exact structural static-output-level test. If Φ(L)=ΣρⱼLʲ is the growth-lag polynomial, a structural static level channel generally implies B(L)=γ[1−Φ(L)](1−L^h), which can require temperature support beyond h. That nonlinear structural restriction is not estimated or tested here.


For annual growth, the contemporaneous static-level restriction cancels current and previous-year coefficients, with later weather coefficients zero. It does not cancel the current term with an arbitrarily chosen fifth- or tenth-year endpoint. A failure to reject these restrictions can reflect low power or collinearity.
Temperature is measured in °C or differences in °C. Percentage growth of Celsius temperature is inappropriate because its zero is arbitrary. A fixed season-climatology anomaly is also distinct from a YoY difference. In a linear model with the relevant seasonal fixed effects, that anomaly only reparameterizes the exposure.

Exact implementation choices
Output, sector definitions and aggregation
Y is 100 × log change in real output. Complete VA12/20 includes official ownership of dwellings, with earlier 11/19 definitions preserved. National GVA always uses the official real total. National IIP uses the published weighted index. Equal-unit panel coefficients are not weighted aggregate growth effects. Annual VA sums complete quarterly flows; annual IIP averages twelve months. Growth and lags are formed before sample exclusions.
Matched histories and sample loss
Subannual current, unrestricted, dynamic and two-channel models share the same maximum-AR-history eligible dates within context/outcome/variant. Weather histories come from the broader weather calendar; growth and its lags come from complete raw sector output histories before comparison-panel truncation. Explicitly matched cuts also intersect the target definition’s required-history eligibility. Missing raw history, target-calendar alignment and crisis exclusions are reported separately; no raw outcome is imputed. A current-only benchmark can therefore have fewer dates here than its original full-history counterpart. Context parents already embody their labelled current-outcome/matched-date definition; the loss table describes subsequent history, coverage and alignment steps.
Annual models report both their own weather-history eligibility and a common ten-year-weather-history comparison set. These are declared comparison calendars, not significance-selected samples. Missing weather histories and insufficient residual time rank create explicit unavailable equations.
Crisis exclusions follow the complete output support
Broad event/rebound blocks are 1985–1986, 1997–1998, 2001–2003, 2008–2009 and 2020–2022. “Event dates excluded” removes only current dates. For the strict dynamic subannual comparison, short-growth equations require t−h−1 through t to be clean; YoY equations require t−2h through t. Thus lagged growth cannot silently reintroduce crisis-year output. Annual models without lagged Y require current and prior output years clean. The seasonal family includes AR1 comparisons and consistently requires t−2 through t clean across its four models. Historical weather during a crisis is not itself an economic-output outlier.
Which equation families were requested?
Common/H1 subannual models cover current, distributed, dynamic and YoY two-channel variants. Separate-sector equations are requested for the long-history distributed model only; national singleton equations already are time series. Annual separate-sector checks are limited to model-own-history DJO5 and the BHM sector-quadratic-trend model. Seasonal and full wind/PM literature companions request common/H1 equations. Earlier static own-series checks remain in the master report. A selector combination outside this scope is unrequested; an unavailable request remains visible with its rank/time-support reason.
Full wind / PM control companion
Every specification in that branch includes current wind and PM2.5, current humidity and the labelled rainfall history, using the original 90% coverage rule. Past temperature/rainfall and growth lags use their full source histories; missing historical PM is not invented or required when PM enters only currently. Quarterly dynamic equations can be underidentified; all controls stay in the unavailable requests. The main complete-GVA baseline includes all five current weather variables; long-history literature models are explicitly a robustness branch.
Fixed effects and inference
Subannual panels use sector × quarter-of-year or sector × month-of-year FE and common year FE. Separate equations use season and year indicators. H1 interacts every temperature feature with sector, sharing rainfall, humidity, year effects and, where present, growth-lag slopes. Total sector lag effects are βlag + δsector,lag. Annual models use sector intercepts and the labelled trend specification. National weather is common across sectors; full actual-date FE would absorb its common slope.
Requested sector/industry CR1 is reported for common pooled slopes, with calendar Driscoll–Kraay sensitivity. Joint sector profiles, national equations and separate series use calendar HAC. Bartlett weights use actual time gaps. Reported finite-sample corrections and inference degrees of freedom are saved with the machine-readable estimates. Pointwise sector effects cannot obtain independent replication merely by adding sector rows.
Interpretation and scientific limits
A temperature-lag coefficient is conditional on other weather terms and, in the dynamic model, past growth. The cumulative direct coefficient uses all lag covariances. It is not an unconditional long-run multiplier in models containing lagged Y. A finite-horizon sum also cannot establish a permanent climate effect. A quadratic marginal effect uses the linear/quadratic cross covariance and is reported within observed temperature support only.
Growth is the primary outcome, but that does not prove stationarity. Existing ADF/KPSS diagnostics remain diagnostic screens; short or gapped exact windows are not certified. We do not delete observations or choose transformations to obtain a desired test result. Unit-specific dynamic equations are not bias-corrected estimators. Shared national shocks, pollution as a possible mediator, changing economic composition, serial persistence, high-dimensional joint-test conditioning and post-exploration multiple testing remain threats to interpretation.
All available p-values are nominal and conditional. Rank-deficient restrictions have no p-value. High condition numbers, many restrictions relative to weather dates, and small residual time degrees of freedom are flagged even if a test is computable. No claim of “AEA-ready causal evidence” follows from passing code checks.
