Singapore · destination weather · origin heterogeneity

Heat & international visitor arrivals

Native fixed-effect growth regressions, timing checks, annual aggregation and zero-retaining count models. The original analysis remains available in the master report.

These are conditional associations from one destination weather history. More origin rows do not create independent Singapore-weather observations.

All eligible, event exclusion and full-support exclusion

H1 is the main origin-heterogeneity equation: temperature plus temperature × origin, with shared other controls and year effects. A reported origin effect is the main coefficient plus its interaction deviation. Existing fully origin-specific equations are a separately labelled robustness analysis with different nuisance assumptions.

In the matched origin × year FE comparison, the equal-origin mean of the temperature point slopes is unchanged by balanced-panel algebra. Individual slopes and their uncertainty can change. Agreement of that mean is not independent confirmation of a national temperature effect.

Exact effects, significance and ordinary growth

“Yes” is a nominal pointwise p below 0.05; “No” does not establish zero effect. Pointwise HAC p-values and intervals are working approximations too: selected conditional overlap benchmarks show undercoverage, especially for YoY or very thin windows. Those benchmark laws do not supply empirical replacement critical values. Holm p adjusts the displayed origin profile for one feature/sum and specification, not the full research grid. Growth means/SD use exactly the reported estimation sample. PPML means are arrival counts and its coefficients have separate log-mean units.

Shared controls, lagged growth and base/interaction components

Interaction deviations compare an origin with the reference. They are not the total temperature effect. Reference deviations are normalized to zero, without an estimated standard error.

Joint restrictions

Multi-restriction Wald p-values are nominal asymptotic diagnostics. Full covariance rank establishes computability, not finite-sample calibration. Selected arrivals diagnostics now show the same problem directly: for two long-history MoM H1 designs, nominal 5% joint tests reject 26.1% / 48.7% of 4,000 Gaussian-null draws for nine regions at HAC12/24, and 100% at both bandwidths for 42 named markets. Every draw passes the declared numerical rank/HC0 gates. These are fixed-design reference-law counterexamples, not the empirical error law of observed arrivals, a universal restriction-count cutoff, or a replacement p-value rule. Do not treat these omnibus p-values as headline evidence of a mechanism or heterogeneity.

What is counted, and what adds up?

The source counts monthly international arrival events by place of residence, not unique tourists or necessarily sovereign countries. Malaysian citizens arriving by land are excluded by the source definition. The frozen M550001 metadata contain an adjustment-flag discrepancy; primary values are the documented NSA count series, with the separately published SA national comparison preserved in the original analysis. The official source has 225 months and 62 published rows. Nine regions omit top-level Others; 42 named markets omit residual buckets. Ten broad buckets and 52 leaf buckets form exhaustive partitions. Their count identities are checked for every source month. Equal-origin log growth is a different estimand from growth of the national total.

Reported zero counts remain zero. Log-growth comparisons explicitly require positive endpoints and balanced dates across the selected cuts. No arbitrary constant is added. PPML checks the selected and exhaustive partitions while retaining eligible zero observations; native separation and sample retention are audited.

Temperature, rainfall and humidity use the official Changi monthly series. Wind is the available-station half-hour mean converted from knots to m/s; PM2.5 is the hourly mean over five regions, with hour-ending timestamps aligned before monthly aggregation. Full-control dates require at least 90% temporal coverage for each added control. These spatial measurements are different; temperature is not an all-station Singapore average.

Which identities are checked?

For shared-weather PPML with origin × season and common year FE, exhaustive component intensities sum to the aggregate model. Matching complete-partition coefficients and calendar covariance is therefore a model-specific aggregation check, not independent confirmation of a temperature effect. Selected nine/42-origin cuts are different estimands.

Where dates go

Current output support, raw lag history, cross-cut alignment, missing controls, coverage and fixed crisis exclusions are separate causes of sample loss. Raw growth and lags are formed before filtering. The matched-current, distributed, dynamic and YoY-channel comparisons share maximum-history dates; the own-history current baseline retains its own broader eligible sample.

Exact-calendar ADF / KPSS screens

Ordinary marginal screens do not prove stationarity, causal identification or correct regression-test size. Gaps are not compressed. Annual growth has fewer than twenty complete years and cannot pass the declared minimum-history screen. PPML count-level screens are descriptive; they are not a universal stationarity prerequisite for a Poisson conditional-mean model.

Independent native Stata checks

Key arrival specifications are re-estimated through the real Stata MCP with reghdfe. Native samples, coefficients and default VCE are preserved separately from the project’s calendar covariance in Mata. Each is compared with a separately executed Python port. Unit-clustered H1 covariance can be singular; unsupported p-values are not printed as significant.

The fixed subset includes current-growth baselines and origin interactions, plus two actual-date-FE relative-effect checks. It does not claim that every timing/count robustness equation was re-estimated in Stata.

Exact protocol and interpretation

Monthly Y is 100 times a log difference in NSA arrivals: one month or twelve months. A +0.1°C current-temperature comparison changes the fitted growth outcome by 0.1β log points, conditional on the other regressors. A distributed coefficient sum concerns a uniform change across its included weather history. It is not an unconditional long-run multiplier when lagged growth enters.

PPML estimates the log conditional mean of count levels; its temperature readout reports 100β log points per °C. An exact +0.1°C expected-count percentage is 100[exp(0.1β)−1], using the unscaled link coefficient β. This is not a monthly growth effect or a count of lost visitors.

Annual arrivals sum complete twelve-month source counts before any monthly log eligibility filter. Temperature/humidity are day weighted and rain is summed. Annual origin FE and a common linear trend retain temporal variation; full year FE would absorb common annual weather. The short Singapore history does not replicate an AEA cross-country design.

The monthly working calendar correction is T/(T−K_time), where K_time is the maximum local design rank across origins, with approximate t(T−1) inference. The complete cross-origin temporal-span rank is reported separately. It is not a point-estimation guard. Sector/origin CR1 is a pooled comparison under independent-origin assumptions; one common destination exposure and one supporting origin do not provide independent exposure replication.

Contemporaneous PM2.5 may respond to weather or economic activity; conditioning on it does not identify total heat damage. Travel can be booked before realised weather is observed. Origin income/prices, exchange rates, airline capacity, events, restrictions and origin weather remain potential confounders. Realised monthly destination weather cannot by itself establish long-term climate damage or tourism-revenue losses.
