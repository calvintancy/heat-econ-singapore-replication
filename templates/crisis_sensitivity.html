Heat, complete GVA and crisis sensitivity

Native PyFixest regressions, complete sector definitions and explicit sample comparisons. Existing 11/19-sector results remain available; new complete 12/20-component panels include ownership of dwellings.

These are exploratory conditional associations. Numerical verification does not establish causal identification, stationarity or conference acceptance.

Exact effects and significance

“Yes” means a nominal, pointwise p-value below 0.05. A “No” is not evidence of zero impact. H1 FE sector rows report total effects βT + δᵢ; separate-series rows report their own temperature slopes. Holm p-values, where supplied, adjust the displayed sector family only; they do not adjust the complete research grid.

Small calendar samples and changes to the crisis window can materially change estimates. Ordinary ADF/KPSS tests cannot establish stationarity; gapped crisis samples are not treated as contiguous series. Levels remain descriptive. Joint tests can be unavailable or ill-conditioned even when individual regressions are identified.

What is Y?

QoQ / MoM log growth100 × [ln real output(t) − ln real output(t−1)], using seasonally adjusted (SA) output. A coefficient of −1 means one log point lower period growth for a 1°C increase, conditional on controls and FE.
YoY log growth100 × [ln real output(t) − ln real output(t−h)], h=4 quarters or 12 months. NSA is the primary YoY definition; SA is retained as a separate comparison. Log points approximate percentage growth but are not exactly ordinary percentage changes.
Annual log growth100 × [ln annual real output(t) − ln annual real output(t−1)]. VA sums four complete quarterly flows; annual IIP averages twelve monthly indices. This differs from a quarterly YoY observation.
Levels100 × ln SA output, or raw SA output. VA units are chained-2015 S$ million; IIP units are 2025=100 index points. These equations are descriptive and are not cleared by having fixed effects.

Fixed effects and heterogeneity

Monthly/quarterly panels include sector × calendar-season FE and common year FE. Sector × quarter FE means one intercept for each sector and quarter-of-year combination, not a separate effect for every actual calendar quarter. Annual models use sector intercepts and a common linear trend, with no year FE. Full date FE absorb the common national temperature coefficient.

The H1 FE model estimates temperature plus temperature × sector interactions, with shared other-weather slopes and year effects. Each displayed sector effect is βT + δᵢ, with Var(βT) + Var(δᵢ) + 2Cov(βT,δᵢ). A deviation δᵢ alone compares the sector with the reference. Separate series are robustness equations with their own weather slopes and deterministic terms.

Sample policy

The fixed event blocks are 1985–1986, 1997–1998, 2001–2003, 2008–2009 and 2020–2022, including broad recovery buffers. These are analyst-chosen sensitivity windows, not official recession dates. No observation is removed because its growth rate, residual or p-value looks unusual.

“Crisis dates excluded” removes current-period observations only. “Strict growth window clean” requires every period between the current and year-earlier outcome to avoid the blocks. Annual growth requires both current and prior years to be clean. Growth is computed before exclusions, using actual calendar predecessors. All five subannual outcomes share the same strict dates within each context.

Inference and effective replication

Common pooled slopes report sector/industry CR1 as requested, with calendar Driscoll–Kraay alternatives. National and sector-specific effects use calendar HAC; a slope supported by one sector cannot obtain reliable inference from resampling sectors. Actual calendar gaps are preserved. The number of sector rows is not the number of independent weather realizations.

Complete coverage is distinct from additivity

Matched dates mean the compared regressions use the same calendar periods. Complete component coverage means the sector list includes ownership of dwellings and covers the accounting partition. Neither implies that real chain-linked components sum exactly to the published total.

The complete broad partition contains 12 components; the detailed partition contains 20. Both include the official ownership-of-dwellings series. The 11/19-unit definitions are retained for comparison. All regressions use real chained-2015 VA. Nominal current-price VA is used only for the accounting identity check.

In 2026Q2, the complete broad nominal components sum to S$203,824.0 million, exactly the published GVA. Real NSA components sum to S$147,017.4 million versus the official S$147,466.9 million (−0.305%). Real SA components sum to S$147,799.4 million versus S$148,025.0 million (−0.152%). Chain-linking and direct seasonal adjustment explain why complete real component sums need not be exact. See the existing aggregate-checks chapter for all dates and source reconciliation.

Official aggregate GVA equations use the published total. Official IIP-total equations use the published weighted index. Panel common slopes are equal-component averages; they are not value-added-weighted aggregates. Manufacturing clusters and subindustries retain the existing official IIP weight and index reconciliation checks.

Weather

The long weather branch uses the cached NEA Changi temperature, rainfall and humidity histories; it is not an all-station spatial average. Quarterly temperature and humidity average monthly observations with calendar-day weights; rainfall sums the months. Wind and PM2.5 retain their separate source coverage and documented aggregation. The complete-GVA baseline with all five controls has 29 eligible quarters, 19 after event-date exclusion, and 17 with the strict growth-window rule.

The verification receipts check numerical, source and calendar consistency. They do not convert the crisis sensitivity into a preregistered design or prove an effect is causal.
