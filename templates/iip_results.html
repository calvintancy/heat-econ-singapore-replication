__FREQUENCY_SECTIONS__
Definitions, sources & identification

What changes with frequency.

Monthly IIP; quarterly averages of levels

IIP is a monthly real manufacturing production index, rather than value added. The current published series is rebased to 2025=100 and classified under SSIC 2025. EDB uses chain-linked Laspeyres indices with annually updated value-added weights; the current linked history is used without manually splicing retired bases.

The balanced six-cluster panel begins with levels in January 1992, giving first monthly growth in February 1992 and first quarterly growth in 1992Q2. It includes electronics, chemicals, biomedical manufacturing, precision engineering, transport engineering and general manufacturing. The detailed 18-industry panel begins with levels in January 1996, giving first growth in February / 1996Q2. Parent and child industries are never pooled together.

Quarterly IIP levels are the arithmetic mean of three monthly index levels, then log growth is calculated. Quarterly seasonally adjusted IIP is constructed from the monthly SA series. Published quarterly NSA IIP provides a check: __SOURCE_RECONCILIATION__ This is distinct from averaging monthly growth rates. Every retained quarter has all three months.

Fixed effects and uncertainty

Monthly models use sector × month-of-year and year fixed effects; quarterly models use sector × quarter-of-year and year effects. Monthly weather uses the published Changi monthly mean air temperature, total rainfall and mean relative humidity. Quarterly temperature and humidity means are day-weighted; rainfall is summed. Common-slope pooled models use sector-clustered CR1 covariance, with six industry clusters and t(5) inference. Bartlett Driscoll–Kraay benchmarks use 12/24 monthly or four/eight quarterly lags. A contemporaneous period-clustered comparison is also reported.

The sector-CR1 correction is S/(S−1) × (N−1)/(N−K), with t(S−1), where S is the number of sectors. The calendar-HAC comparison uses T/(T−1) × (N−1)/(N−K), with t(T−1): N is sector-period observations, T is observed periods and K includes fixed-effect coefficients. Actual calendar gaps are retained when excluding 2020–2022. A 1°C coefficient is a scale convention larger than a typical residual temperature movement.

Interpretation

DJO log-growth form: these 100 × log-difference outcomes adapt Dell–Jones–Olken to monthly/quarterly frequency, not its annual multicountry design. A coefficient of −1 means about one percentage point lower period growth per +1°C, conditional on controls and fixed effects; it does not establish a permanent growth loss.

A common temperature series provides one weather observation per month or quarter, replicated across industries. The panel uses equal sector weights, so it does not estimate the official aggregate manufacturing response. Full year-month or year-quarter fixed effects would absorb common national temperature entirely. Annual effects leave within-year macro demand and industry shocks as potential confounders; these models do not identify causal heat damage or permanent climate change impacts.
