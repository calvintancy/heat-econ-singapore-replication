Read the +0.1°C columnIt is one tenth of the regression coefficient and interval. Compare it with that row’s own mean growth; monthly and quarterly responses are different estimands. It is a conditional fitted association, not a causal prediction. A negative or near-zero mean makes “percent of average growth” comparisons misleading, so no such ratio is shown.

QoQ/MoM = 100[ln A(t) − ln A(t−1)]; YoY = 100[ln A(t) − ln A(t−h)], with h=4 quarters or 12 months. These are log growth rates, not annualised. YoY results retain both NSA and SA output versions; short-period growth uses SA output. Both growth horizons use Dell–Jones–Olken’s log-difference outcome form, adapted to our frequency; this is not its full annual multicountry design. A growth coefficient β=−1 means about one percentage point lower growth per +1°C, conditional on controls and FE; it does not by itself establish a permanent growth loss. Means are signed arithmetic averages of those log growth observations. Aggregate VA is gross value added at basic prices, including ownership of dwellings; it is distinct from GDP including product taxes and from the average across our 11-sector panel.

Definitions, evidence and source notes.

Growth is the primary macroeconomic outcome. The AEA study by Dell, Jones and Olken uses 100 times annual log differences in GDP and sector value added, confirmed from its replication code. Our monthly/quarterly analyses use that transformation at their respective frequencies. Temperature remains the period’s mean air temperature unless a column explicitly uses another exposure definition.

What changes in the aggregate specification?

Aggregate VA comes directly from SingStat’s published real gross value added at basic prices. Chain-linked sector levels are not added together. There is one observation per quarter, so its fixed effects are quarter-of-year and year. Sector models use sector-specific seasonality; monthly IIP uses month-of-year seasonality. Full date fixed effects absorb common national weather; the relative heterogeneity model identifies sector differences.

Which temperature significance test?

A sector’s reported effect is βT + δᵢ (the reference has δ=0 by normalization). Its p-value tests the one restriction βT + δᵢ=0, using Var(βT)+Var(δᵢ)+2Cov(βT,δᵢ). The separate joint heterogeneity test asks whether all nonreference δᵢ are zero, with the common βT left unrestricted. Relative R1 coefficients test differences from the reference only. Singular omnibus covariance is never inverted to manufacture a p-value.

Has stationarity been established?

No universal clearance is claimed. The expanded Stationarity tab maps monthly/quarterly source-model outcomes and stochastic weather regressors, including detailed sectors, national totals, SA/NSA YoY, exact growth, wind/PM2.5 and transformed weather. Annual diagnostics are reported in the Annual analyses tab. Primary ADF uses a two-year lag cap; primary KPSS uses uncapped automatic bandwidth, with the legacy one-year settings retained as sensitivity. Mixed/lag-sensitive evidence remains visible. Standard tests are not run on compressed gapped calendars. Full-source evidence does not clear an untestable short regression window. All significance labels remain nominal conditional inference.

Which errors are clustered?

Common-slope panels cluster at sector i: 11 broad or 19 detailed VA sectors, six IIP clusters or 18 detailed manufacturing industries. The sector-CR1 correction is S/(S−1) × (N−1)/(N−K), with t(S−1) inference. Six clusters is a small number; this covariance permits within-sector dependence but does not account for dependence across sectors from common national shocks. Calendar-aware Driscoll–Kraay comparisons remain in the report. Aggregate and fully sector-specific coefficients retain time-series or joint calendar HAC because sector clustering is unavailable or degenerate.

Annual frequency and the AEA comparison

The Annual analyses tab retains the monthly/quarterly YoY results and adds annual NSA growth with SA alongside. Calendar-year VA sums four quarterly flow levels; IIP averages twelve monthly levels. Annual temperature and humidity are day-weighted; rainfall is summed. The annual Singapore models use sector FE and a linear time trend. Year FE would absorb the common national annual temperature series, so this is an annual-frequency/log-growth analogue of Dell–Jones–Olken with distinct single-country identification.

How to read the COVID comparison

The short sample adds wind and PM2.5 to rainfall and humidity, under the same 90% monthly coverage rules. The “exclude 2020–2022” version removes endpoint observations dated in those years, preserving actual calendar gaps. A YoY observation dated 2023 still has a 2022 base period, so that exclusion is not a claim of a completely pandemic-free outcome window.

The short quarterly sample has only 29 observed periods, falling to 19 after the exclusion. Covariance and unit-root diagnostics have finite-sample limits. Levels are retained as descriptive sensitivities because of persistence concerns. The notes below preserve the complete literature review, transformations, model choices, diagnostics and verification evidence.
