Sector heterogeneity

Do sectors respond differently?

Estimate each sector’s temperature slope, test their differences jointly, and compare the restrictions imposed by pooled and separate regressions. Growth is the main outcome; persistent output levels are a descriptive sensitivity.

Y is labelled explicitly. MoM/QoQ growth = 100[ln A(t) − ln A(t−1)]; YoY growth = 100[ln A(t) − ln A(t−h)], where h is 12 months or four quarters. Log level = 100 ln A(t). Output is seasonally adjusted; growth is not annualised.

Every model here uses the current month/quarter’s mean air temperature in °C. Absolute slopes refer to growth log points, or output log points for levels, per 1°C. The date-FE model identifies differences from a reference sector. These are conditional associations, not established causal heat effects.

Significance is explicit. *** and “Significant at 1%” mean p<0.01; ** and “Significant at 5%” mean 0.01≤p<0.05; * and “10% only” mean 0.05≤p<0.10; otherwise “Not significant at 10%”. A 5%: Yes badge means p<0.05. Labels use unrounded saved p-values, rather than rounded interval endpoints. Individual-sector tests are pointwise; joint equality tests and Holm-adjusted selected pairwise tests have separate labels.

Significance is nominal conditional inference; unresolved stationarity means exploratory. Y and X flags refer to the exact estimation window. Ordinary ADF/KPSS screens cannot prove stationarity; screens for calendar gaps or short histories are unavailable. Full source-history background is shown separately and is not clearance of the displayed window. Levels remain descriptive sensitivities.

__PANELS__

What the comparisons identify

All outcomes use the same sectors and dates within a context and control set: 11 broad VA sectors or six manufacturing clusters. The long samples contain 178 VA quarters, 405 IIP months or 135 IIP quarters. The IIP comparisons begin in 1993 because every sector needs a year-ago output level. Parent and child industries are not mixed. Raw-level regressions remain in the separate growth-and-levels report; this heterogeneity report uses proportional outcomes for cross-sector comparability.

The baseline uses rainfall, relative humidity, wind speed and PM2.5 on every retained quality-screened date in 2017–2025. COVID dates are included in the baseline. The endpoint exclusion and the longer sample without wind/PM2.5 are robustness checks. In H1, Yᵢₜ = βᵢTₜ + γ₁rainₜ + γ₂RHₜ + γ₃windₜ + γ₄PM2.5ₜ + sector × season FE + common year FE + εᵢₜ. H1 allows sector-specific temperature slopes with those shared weather controls, shared year effects and sector-specific seasonality. H2 allows every weather slope to vary by sector. H3 additionally makes year effects sector-specific. With identical dates and regressors, H3 reproduces separate OLS point estimates. The jointly estimated covariance is needed to test differences between sectors; separate confidence intervals cannot substitute for that covariance.

R1 includes full date effects, sector × year and sector × season effects, with weather interactions for all nonreference sectors. Common temperature and other common weather terms are absorbed. With this balanced design, R1 recovers H3’s slope differences exactly; it is a useful identification and interpretation check rather than an additional independent result. The reference is manufacturing for VA and electronics for IIP.

Inference and the short weather window

Covariance sums sector scores by actual calendar date, then applies Bartlett Driscoll–Kraay HAC with four/eight quarterly or 12/24 monthly lags. The correction is T/(T−1) × (NT−1)/(NT−K), where T is observed dates and K includes all fitted parameters. Individual tests use approximate t(T−1); equality tests use approximate F(q,T−1). Stars and sector intervals are pointwise. The selected difference tests also report Holm-adjusted p-values within each model, outcome and bandwidth. No adjustment across every specification is claimed.

Weather has one national observation per date, repeated across sectors. The full-control samples contain only 29 quarters or 100 months. Fully flexible quarterly equations have 12 residual time degrees of freedom, falling to five after excluding 2020–2022 endpoints. The VA omnibus test after that exclusion has ten restrictions but covariance rank at most five, so no p-value is reported. Singular tests are not silently inverted with a pseudoinverse. Even full-rank tests can be fragile when the sample is short and the restriction covariance is ill conditioned.

Why stationarity is not confirmed for the baseline window

The baseline has missing calendar dates after the wind/PM2.5 quality screen. Ordinary ADF/KPSS tests assume evenly spaced observations; deleting the gaps and treating the survivors as consecutive would change the time series. Its exact-window screens are therefore unavailable, rather than failed stationarity tests. Contiguous source-history tests provide background evidence but do not certify this gapped regression window. A short history or sensitivity across lag choices can leave other screens unresolved. ADF has a unit-root null; KPSS has a level- or trend-stationarity null. Tests use exact contiguous long-sample dates, plus a pre-2020 contiguous sensitivity. Official SA output is used directly. Log-level tests include a constant and trend; growth tests include a constant. Weather has estimated month/quarter seasonal offsets removed while retaining its trend; temperature and humidity use trend tests, rainfall uses constant tests. Temperature also has a constant-only sensitivity in the downloads. Weather critical values after seasonal adjustment are approximate diagnostic screens.

ADF selects lags by AIC up to one year; KPSS uses its automatic bandwidth capped at one year. Lag choices, boundary p-values and warnings are saved. Many KPSS p-values are bounded by its lookup table; < and > are preserved. Lag-cap choices, breaks, finite-sample power and overlapping YoY windows affect interpretation. Non-rejection does not prove stationarity or a unit root. Neither raw-series tests nor residual correlations establish cointegration, causal identification or the adequacy of year effects.

The wind/PM2.5 quality-screened sample has calendar gaps: seven missing quarters or eight missing months. It is not compressed and passed to ordinary ADF/KPSS. Residual correlations instead match the actual calendar lags. Humidity tests can indicate persistence or break sensitivity without mechanically invalidating every year-FE regression; further dynamic specifications would change the maintained model. __DIAGNOSTIC_NOTE__.
