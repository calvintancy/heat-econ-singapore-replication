Current native Wald inference

The primary joint-test reference here is χ²(q), computed by the native PyFixest Wald kernel for the explicit recorded restriction. Coefficients, samples and the original sector-CR1 or calendar-HAC covariance are preserved. Native test kernel does not mean native default covariance. Coefficient t-tests and their intervals below remain unchanged.

A reference change is not a calibration cure. These are nominal asymptotic p-values. Full rank permits computation; it does not establish a reliable finite-sample reference, causal identification or a mechanism. Selected fixed-design null checks show severe multi-restriction miscalibration. Singular or otherwise guarded tests remain unavailable.

What is native, what is preserved, and what remains uncertain?

The Wald statistic is W = (Rβ − r)′[RVR′]⁻¹(Rβ − r). Its current nominal p-value uses χ² with q restrictions. V is the original saved joint covariance, including the declared clustering, calendar gaps, bandwidth and finite-sample scaling. No regression was refitted for this presentation. The historical comparison reports W/q against its original F(q, df) reference. A one-constraint χ² test can therefore differ from the retained coefficient t-test for the same null.

Equality of sector effects tests their differences; it is distinct from testing every effect against zero. A total sector effect uses βT + δᵢ and the full covariance cross term. Dynamic shape tests remain conditional coefficient restrictions, not automatic structural mechanism tests. Repeated aliases and alternative covariances are not independent confirmations. Holm adjustments on retained coefficient profiles do not adjust this entire joint-test inventory.

Where a coefficient and covariance came from Stata, the new test kernel is PyFixest applied to that preserved estimate/covariance. It is not a new Stata test or a native PyFixest covariance estimate. The source identity for a selected test is available in its details and CSV export.
